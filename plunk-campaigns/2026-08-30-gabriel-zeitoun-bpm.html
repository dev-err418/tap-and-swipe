A run club became a €1M/year dating app

Hey, it's Arthur :)

I sat down with Gabriel Zeitoun, the founder of BPM, a dating app for sporty singles.

BPM reached €1M in annualized revenue in 4.5 months, launching only in France. That's its revenue pace projected over a year.

Dating apps have an awkward launch problem. You can build the whole thing, but if the first person opens it and finds nobody to meet, why would they stay?

Gabriel started with a run club for singles in Paris. People met, relationships started, and he built a community he could bring into the app when it launched.

Some notes I took from the interview:

  • The waiting list had around 4,000 people. It brought roughly 500 installs over the first weekend. Even with a community, you still have to turn interest into people actually using the app.
  • The free app stays usable. The two main reasons people subscribe are sending more likes and seeing who liked their profile. Free members also make the app more valuable for paying members.
  • They reviewed every profile by hand at first, sometimes up to 500 a day. Gabriel wanted to protect the experience from fake accounts and poor profiles. Around 90% of that process is automated now.
  • He started Meta ads at €50/day. Before raising the budget, he'd spend one to three weeks checking that performance held up and the team could produce enough working creatives.
  • He reports a 14-day payback on ad spend, measured through net ROAS. That helped him scale without waiting months to find out whether acquisition was paying back.

"The product is actually the users."

I liked that he found a way to test the idea before the app existed.

He wanted to help active singles meet. Organizing a run let him do exactly that, see whether people enjoyed it, and give them a reason to come back. By launch, he already had people to invite.
