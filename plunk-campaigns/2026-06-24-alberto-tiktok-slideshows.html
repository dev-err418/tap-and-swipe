100,000 app downloads from TikTok slideshows

Hey, it's Arthur :)

I just published a new Tap & Swipe episode with Alberto Pérez.

Alberto is an app founder who grew Flowfy, a budget tracking app, with TikTok slideshows.

No paid ads. No UGC actors. No polished video production setup.

Just simple slideshow posts, repeated a lot.

The result: 100,000+ app downloads, around 2-5M TikTok views a month, and about $8K/month across his two main apps.

The part I liked most is that Alberto does not treat content as something you add after the app is finished.

He thinks about the content while building the app. The product needs at least one screen, result, or visual moment that can make someone stop scrolling and ask, "what app is this?"

Some notes I took from the interview:

  • Slideshows work because they are repeatable. A format you can make every day is usually more useful than a "perfect" video you can only make twice.
  • On a new account, Alberto tests five or six content formats first. If one gets around 15K views, he treats it as a signal and starts making variations.
  • The content should not feel like an ad. The best posts make people feel like they discovered the app themselves.
  • A viral post with no downloads is still a failed app post. The app has to be connected to the reason people watched.
  • He starts where he has a natural advantage. Spain is easier for him than the US because the language, phone, culture, and content instincts are native.
  • Music and hashtags matter, but not as much as founders want them to. The format is the strategy. The song is not.

"When you find a video that goes viral with your type of content and you don't spend too much time on it, that's the video that you have to replicate over and over and over again."

That line is simple, but it changes the whole game.

A lot of founders ask, "what content could go viral?" Alberto's version is better: "what content could go viral that I can actually repeat without burning out?"

The full episode gets into the messy parts: how he avoids looking like an ad, how he scales accounts into other countries, why he edits inside TikTok instead of automating everything, how he prices Flowfy, and why he thinks builders should plan distribution before they build.
